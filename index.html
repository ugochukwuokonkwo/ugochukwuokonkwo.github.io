Flagship case study

Human-Centered Redesign of an Assistive Button Hook

Needs assessment → concepts → CAD → prototypes → fixtures → preliminary usability evaluation

Challenge

Conventional button hooks may still require sustained gripping, controlled wrist motion and precise wire-loop positioning. I led a two-aim redesign focused on a more supportive handle and a refined hook mechanism for easier engagement, retention and release.

My contribution

Requirements development, concept generation, Creo modeling, prototype fabrication, clinician-informed iteration, wire-forming fixture development, device integration, test planning and usability-data analysis.

7handle concepts
5hook concepts
4revised fixtures
30participants

Engineering decisions

  • Translated literature, commercial benchmarking, clinician input, button dimensions and hand-size accommodation into design criteria and fabrication constraints.
  • Selected an angled, palm-supported handle with finger grooves, padding and an adjustable pivoting strap.
  • Designed wire-forming molds and jigs to improve alignment, repeatability and control of springback.
  • Integrated a refined 1 mm stainless spring-wire loop with the selected handle and retention system.

Preliminary results

  • 96% reported that the strap improved grip.
  • 97% found the strap easy to adjust.
  • 93% reported a stable grip and comfortable wrist position.
  • 90% reported smooth wire-loop movement.
  • 84% reported secure button retention, identifying retention and release as priorities for continued refinement.
Study scope: Thirty PT/OT graduate students completed a standardized buttoning task and an adapted QUEST 2.0 survey. This was a preliminary usability evaluation—not a clinical-effectiveness study or a direct commercial-device comparison.

Computational project

Spinal Cord Thermal Distribution Modeling

Multilayer model → transient simulation → mesh independence → thermal response

Research question

Could controlled surface heating produce spinal-cord thermal effects relevant to electrical-stimulation-based pain-modulation research?

Tools

ANSYS Fluent, transient thermal analysis, bioheat modeling, mesh independence studies, MATLAB and engineering data analysis.

Method

  • Created a multilayer model including spinal cord, CSF, epidural fat, dura/bone and skin-fat-muscle layers.
  • Applied literature-derived thermal properties and a 45°C external boundary condition.
  • Compared 0.4, 0.2 and 0.1 mm meshes.

Finding

The model predicted an approximately 1.78°C spinal-cord temperature increase after about eight minutes of controlled heating. The result supports further investigation; it does not establish clinical efficacy.
